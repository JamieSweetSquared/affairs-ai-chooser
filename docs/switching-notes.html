Switching from free / default tools

Migration cost framing when you outgrow a free host form, a Notion page, or a default ESP — not a case-study gallery. Research-labeled decision notes. Not a hands-on review.

Who this is for

You already have something (Carrd, Notion, Google Forms, a free ESP plan, a WP theme) and you are wondering whether to consolidate into a funnel + email all-in-one or keep stitching tools. You want cost and effort honesty — not invented “we migrated and made $X” stories.

Migration cost frame (no fake case studies)

General ops framing. Not a lab test of any vendor’s importer.

  1. List export / import. Expect CSV export from the old ESP, field mapping, and a double-opt-in or re-permission check if the list is cold. Time: often hours for a small list; days if tags/automations are messy.
  2. Page rebuild. Landing and sales pages rarely “port.” Budget a rewrite pass in the new builder (or keep DIY URLs and only move email).
  3. Automations rewrite. Sequences, tags, and webhooks usually do not transfer 1:1. Recreate the minimum path (welcome → nurture → offer) first.
  4. DNS / domain. Custom domains and email authentication (SPF/DKIM) add a half-day for many solos — plan it before you cut over.
  5. Opportunity cost. A week of migration is a week not shipping product. If the free stack still converts, delay the switch.

When switching is worth considering

  • You hit hard caps (contacts, funnels, or branding) on a free plan and the next paid tier elsewhere is worse value for your job.
  • You are paying for separate email + pages + checkout and want one dashboard — see all-in-one vs stack.
  • Launch velocity matters more than perfect DIY control — see funnel builder vs DIY.

When to stay put

  • No offer and no consistent traffic — tooling will not invent demand.
  • Your free/default stack still supports the only funnel step you need.
  • You need specialist depth (creator ESP polish, SEO scoring, agency CRO) more than consolidation — see sibling FAQs and the ≤$100 stack guide.
